Forecast Methodology

Formulas & methodology

How every number on the forecast page is produced. Constants marked rule of thumb are judgment calls, not fit to data; sourced ones come from published research. Code: github.com/justinwei2/opensnow.

1. Forecast points

Each resort has three named points on the mountain: base, mid-mountain and summit. They're snapped to Open-Meteo's 90 m terrain model, and the published lift elevation is passed explicitly, because the terrain model smooths peaks. Requests use the nearest model grid cell. Open-Meteo's default can jump several km to a cell of similar height, which is how you end up with town weather.

  • Temperature, wet-bulb, snow fraction and snow ratio are computed at each point's elevation.
  • Precipitation is the model grid cell's value. No free model resolves individual ridges (cells are 2.5–25 km).
  • Summit wind uses the free-air wind interpolated to summit height from the pressure levels, not the model's 10 m wind, which is smoothed over terrain. Base and mid use the 10 m wind.

2. Precipitation blend

Up to eight models: ECMWF IFS 9 km and 0.25°, ECMWF AIFS, GFS + HRRR, NBM 2.5 km, GEM, ICON, plus HRDPS 2.5 km in Canada and the northern US. For each hour t:

Pblend(t) = Σm wm · fm · Pm(t)  /  Σm wm

fm = the model's bias factor and wm = its weight (§3). The weight depends on lead time: one set for the next 3.5 days, another (fit on lead days 4–7) beyond that. The sum covers only models with data at that hour, so the blend renormalises as short-range models drop out: HRDPS after 2 days, ICON after 7, GEM after 10, NBM after 11.

3. Calibration

One-time, per resort, against a nearby automated gauge (NRCS SNOTEL or California CDEC). It uses forecasts as they were actually issued (Open-Meteo Previous Runs API) over winters 2024–25 and 2025–26, so there's no look-ahead.

72 h forecast window starting day D = lead-1 day D + lead-2 day D+1 + lead-3 day D+2
fm = Σ observed / Σ forecastm   (clamped to 0.4–2.0)
wm ∝ 1 / MAEm²,   MAEm = mean |fm·forecast − observed| over 72 h windows (lead days 1–3)
wm4+ ∝ 1 / MAEm,daily²,   daily totals at lead days 4–7 (models that don't reach that far get 0)
temperature bias = mean(station − model daily mean temperature) at the gauge, lead day 1: all days (displayed temperatures) and storm days ≥ 2.5 mm (profile correction, §5)
  • Observed precipitation is the daily gauge increment. When the gauge clearly missed a storm (snow water gain > 0.3" and gauge < half of it), the snowpack's water gain is used instead. Storage gauges cap and freeze; Mammoth Pass caught 12" vs 31" of snow-water gain in 2024–25.
  • Resorts without a gauge (Canada): fm = the median of calibrated resorts in the same region (else all US resorts), with equal weights.
  • Error bins for the range (§4): the observed/forecast ratio for the blend, split by forecast storm size per 72 h: 10–25 mm, 25–50 mm, > 50 mm (each bin needs ≥ 15 windows).

Per-resort status, gauge and error: Calibration coverage (in each resort's Details) and CALIBRATION.md.

4. Likely range (25–75%)

Two independent uncertainty estimates, averaged percentile by percentile (quantile averaging):

qp = ½ · [ forecast · ensemble qp / ensemble q50 + forecast · ratiop(storm size) ]
  • Ensembles: about 190 members (ECMWF ENS 51, AIFS ENS 51, GEFS 31, ICON EPS 40, GEM 21). Each ensemble is bias-corrected with its parent model's fm and weighted equally. Window totals use members covering ≥ 90% of the hours. Liquid is converted to snow with the blend's snow/liquid ratio r for that window, and base/mid are scaled by the blend's elevation/summit liquid ratio.
  • Historical error: ratiop is the p-th percentile of observed/forecast at this resort for storms of that size. Window liquid is normalised to 72 h before picking the bin.
  • The ensembles contribute the shape of the uncertainty (each percentile relative to their median), centred on the blend, because coarse ensembles are often drier than the blend several days out. When the blend or ensemble median is dry, absolute ensemble amounts are used.
  • The headline number is the blend itself, and both ranges are widened if needed so they always include it. The middle-90% range (5–95%) is in each resort's Details.

5. Rain vs snow, snow level, freezing level

An hourly consensus profile averages temperature, humidity, height and wind from ECMWF IFS 0.25°, GFS, GEM and ICON at 10 pressure levels (1000–500 hPa), interpolated linearly in height. Every level's temperature is shifted by the resort's storm-day temperature bias (§3), capped at ±1.5 °C because station siting adds its own bias. This moves the rain/snow split, snow level, freezing level and snow ratio together.

Tw = Stull (2011) wet-bulb from T and RH
snow fraction = 1 / (1 + e(Tw − 0.5 °C) / 0.35)   → 90% at −0.3 °C, 50% at +0.5 °C, 10% at +1.3 °C
  • Snow level = highest height where Tw crosses +0.5 °C (≈ 50% snow). The band on the chart is the range across the four models. sourced Jennings et al. 2018 put the Sierra's 50% rain/snow threshold at ≈ 0.6–1.5 °C air temperature. A wet-bulb of +0.5 °C sits in that range for moist air.
  • Freezing level = highest height where T crosses 0 °C.
  • The daily snow level averages the hours with precipitation, so dry hours don't skew it.

6. Snow ratio (snow-to-liquid ratio, Kuchera method)

Tmax = warmest temperature between the elevation and 500 hPa (≈ 18,000 ft).

if Tmax > −2 °C:   SLR = 12 − 2 · (Tmax + 2)
if Tmax ≤ −2 °C:   SLR = 12 + (−2 − Tmax)
× wind penalty = max(0.7, 1 − 0.0025 · (windkm/h − 30)) for wind > 30 km/h (≈ 19 mph)  rule of thumb
clamped to 4 – 25;   sleet hours (§7) use 2:1;   density = 100 / SLR %

snow (in) = Pblend (mm) × snow fraction × SLR / 25.4

The daily ratio averages the hourly ratios, weighted by how much snow fell each hour.

7. Snow-quality labels

One label per elevation per day, only when there's more than 1 mm of liquid. Checks run top to bottom; the first match wins. Snow fraction is precipitation-weighted.

Heavy/wet comes from warmth near the surface, physically closer to rain. Sleet needs warm air above cold air.

8. Weather report (daily notes)

Lift hours = 8am–4pm resort time. Ridge wind = free-air wind at summit height. Estimated gust = max(model gust, 1.4 × sustained). A "snow day" has ≥ 1" summit snow or ≥ 0.1" liquid. rule of thumb for all thresholds.

Alerts are the NWS's own active alerts for the summit point, in their own words (US only). "Unavailable" means the alert fetch failed. It does not mean there are no alerts.

9. Days, past snowfall & spread charts

  • Ski day = 6am to 6am the next morning, split into morning (6–12), afternoon (12–6pm) and night (6pm–6am). Each part's powder-quality bar uses the same quality rules (§7) on that part's hours.
  • Summary: last 7 ski days, last 24 hours, next 1–5 days (the rest of today plus 4 days) and next 6–10 days.
  • Snow & rain chart: amounts per 6 hours for the next 10 days. Each ensemble member's precipitation is split into snow and rain with the forecast's hourly phase and snow ratio. The middle-50% and middle-90% bands are the members' weighted percentiles for each 6-hour period, widened if needed to include the forecast. Members drop out past their horizon (ICON EPS after ~7.5 days).
  • Model estimate: the same blend and phase/ratio maths applied to the last 7 days of the models' short-range forecasts. An estimate, not a measurement.
  • Station measured: the gauge's daily snow-depth gain. Settling makes this under-read new snow, and the station sits at one elevation (shown).

10. Refresh & reliability

  • GitHub Actions checks hourly and refreshes a resort once a new ECMWF run has landed (ECMWF arrives last of the 6-hourly models, so GFS, ICON and AIFS from the same cycle are already in), or if its data is more than 7 h old: about 4 refreshes a day, within about an hour of each model cycle. Only the region groups with due resorts run, as parallel jobs on separate runners, staggered 90 s apart. About 7k of Open-Meteo's free 10k calls/day.
  • HTTP: 6 attempts with exponential backoff and jitter. Permanent 4xx errors aren't retried. On a rate limit, the region stops and keeps its previous data. Failed resorts get a second pass after 60 s. Mostly-empty forecasts are never published.
  • Publishing: data restore/push 3 attempts, Pages deploy 3 attempts, and failed jobs re-run once automatically. Publishing is refused if the resort count drops.
  • Freshness check (update.py --check) after every refresh, plus a daily outside-in watchdog: a missing forecast or 3+ stale resorts fails the run, which emails the repo owner.

11. Limitations

  • Precipitation is at model-grid resolution. Orographic peaks between grid cells aren't resolved.
  • At high resorts the lowest pressure levels lie below the model terrain, so the snow level may read "below base".
  • Calibration is fit and scored on the same two winters, so the error shown is optimistic. ECMWF IFS 9 km and AIFS have one winter of history.
  • The temperature correction assumes the gauge's storm-day bias holds across the resort's elevations.
  • SNOTEL gauges under-catch snow in wind, so calibrated totals may run low. Canadian factors are borrowed from US resorts.
  • Models historically under-forecast the biggest storms 1–3 days out. Watch the top of the range.
  • Thin warm layers (< ~1,500 ft) are missed by the sleet check.
